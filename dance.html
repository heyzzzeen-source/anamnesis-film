Synopsis

In the films of Edison, one finds an array of dazzling, kinetic displays—acrobatics, feats of skill and dance movements—that arrest our gaze. Over time, these spectacles came to unfold within narrative frameworks, as in the slapstick comedies of Chaplin and Keaton. With the advent of classical Hollywood cinema, such attractions took on diverse forms across the studio genres.

Today’s Hollywood cinema differs greatly from that of the classical era. Yet, viewed at an abstract level, what Hollywood offers its audiences today may not be all that different from what it offered then.
